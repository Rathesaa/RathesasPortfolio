Cybersecurity Essentials: Working Securely from Anywhere

An interactive workplace learning concept exploring how scenario-based learning and AI-powered personalization can help employees recognize cybersecurity risks and practise safer responses.

Instructional Design UX / UI Design AI-Powered Learning Concept
01 / Project Overview

Making cybersecurity easier to understand and apply

Employees handle sensitive information across offices, homes, and public spaces. They need more than policy information: they need opportunities to recognize risks and practise responding to them.

The learning challenge

How might we make cybersecurity training more engaging and responsive to individual learner needs while helping employees apply security practices in everyday situations?

Target Audience

Employees who use digital tools and access organizational information in office, remote, and hybrid work settings.

Learning Format

A self-paced digital learning concept designed to take approximately 15–20 minutes.

Learning Objectives

Learners should be able to recognize phishing indicators, select safer responses, and apply practical security habits.

Design Methods

Scenario-based learning, interactive assessments, instructional feedback, and an adaptive learning prototype.

02 / Instructional Design Strategy

Designing for practical application

The course uses familiar workplace situations to help learners recognize risks, consider their options, and practise making informed decisions.

Authentic workplace scenarios

Situations involving suspicious emails and shared workspaces connect cybersecurity concepts to everyday decisions.

Decision-based activities

Learners choose how they would respond to realistic situations instead of only reading about security rules.

Immediate feedback

Explanations reinforce correct decisions and help address misconceptions.

Knowledge checks

Short questions give learners opportunities to recall and apply key concepts.

Adaptive learning

The concept explores how learner preferences and assessment performance can guide personalized explanations and additional practice.

UNDERSTANDING THE RISKS

Where can security risks happen?

Cybersecurity threats can appear in everyday activities. Understanding common risks is the first step toward preventing them.

Phishing & Social Engineering

Messages designed to trick people into sharing information or taking unsafe actions.

Suspicious Links

Unexpected links or files may lead to fraudulent websites or malicious downloads.

Weak Account Security

Reused or easy-to-guess passwords can make accounts vulnerable.

Unsecured Networks

Public or untrusted networks may expose sensitive information.

Information Exposure

Documents, screens, and conversations can unintentionally expose confidential information.

Unexpected Requests

Someone may impersonate a colleague or manager to create urgency.

Remember

A message that looks familiar is not necessarily legitimate. Verify unexpected requests before taking action.

SCENARIO 01 · PHISHING

A message that needs your attention

Sarah is working remotely when she receives an email claiming her company account will be suspended unless she verifies her information within 30 minutes.

From: support@northstarr-secure.com
Subject: URGENT: Your account will be suspended

Hello Sarah,

We detected an issue with your account. Your access will be suspended in 30 minutes unless you verify your credentials immediately.

Please use the link below to confirm your account:

northstar-account.help/login

Thank you,
Northstar IT Support

Can you spot the warning signs?

  • Look-alike sender: The email domain is slightly different from the expected company domain.
  • Manufactured urgency: The 30-minute deadline pressures Sarah to act quickly.
  • Credential request: The message asks for login information.
  • Suspicious destination: The link does not match an expected company website.
Think before you click.

Urgent language, unexpected credential requests, and unfamiliar links are reasons to stop and verify.

04 / Assessment & Evaluation

How learning could be evaluated

Knowledge Assessment

Review quiz scores and scenario responses to identify understanding of security risks.

Learner Feedback

Collect feedback about the clarity, relevance, accessibility, and usefulness of the experience.

Confidence & Application

Use pre- and post-learning questions to explore whether learners feel more prepared to respond to suspicious situations.

Adaptive Recommendations

Evaluate whether personalized practice and explanations help learners address identified knowledge gaps.

Evaluation note

These are proposed evaluation methods. No organizational pilot, learner study, or measured performance improvement is being claimed.

05 / Design Reflection

Bringing UX and learning design together

This project explores how user experience principles can support effective digital learning. Clear navigation, consistent layouts, accessible interactions, and meaningful feedback help learners focus on the content and understand what to do next.

The adaptive learning concept extends this approach by exploring how learner preferences and performance can influence the type of support and practice they receive.

The goal is not to label learners by a fixed learning style, but to create a flexible experience that responds to their needs while keeping the learning objectives consistent.

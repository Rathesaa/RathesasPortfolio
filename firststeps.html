Project 04 · UX + Employee Learning

FirstSteps: A More Structured Employee Onboarding Experience

A digital onboarding concept designed to help new employees navigate their first 30 days, access essential resources, understand learning priorities, and feel more confident starting a new role.

01 · The challenge

Starting a new role can feel overwhelming

New employees are often introduced to a large amount of information in a short period of time. They may need to learn company policies, meet colleagues, understand internal systems, complete mandatory training, and begin their role-specific responsibilities.

The design problem

How might we create a more organized onboarding experience that helps new employees understand what to do, where to find information, and how to progress through their first month?

Learner challenges

Unclear priorities, information spread across multiple platforms, uncertainty about required training, and difficulty knowing who to contact for help.

Design opportunity

Bring onboarding tasks, learning activities, essential resources, and progress tracking into one accessible employee workspace.

02 · Audience & objectives

Supporting employees as they settle into a new role

The concept is designed for employees joining an organization who need a clear introduction to workplace expectations, systems, people, and learning requirements.

01

New employees

People joining an organization who need to understand their responsibilities, workplace culture, and first steps.

02

Managers

Managers who need a shared view of onboarding priorities, progress, and upcoming conversations.

03

HR and L&D teams

Teams coordinating orientation, required learning, employee resources, and onboarding communications.

Experience objectives

✓Help employees understand their onboarding priorities and next steps.
✓Provide a structured first-30-days learning pathway.
✓Make essential policies, tools, and reference materials easier to find.
✓Make onboarding progress visible to support accountability and confidence.
03 · Instructional strategy

A guided path from orientation to application

Rather than presenting every resource at once, FirstSteps organizes onboarding into manageable stages. Each stage combines information, learning, practical activities, and opportunities to ask questions.

01

Days 1–5 · Get oriented

Understand the organization, meet key contacts, access systems, and complete essential orientation activities.

02

Days 6–15 · Build understanding

Explore role-specific processes, complete required learning, and become familiar with workplace tools and procedures.

03

Days 16–30 · Put learning into practice

Apply new knowledge, practise everyday responsibilities, ask questions, and discuss progress with a manager.

▤

Chunk information into manageable steps

Break onboarding into smaller activities instead of overwhelming employees with a large collection of documents.

◎

Connect learning to real work

Combine formal training with practical tasks, guided exploration, and opportunities to apply new knowledge.

⌕

Provide support at the point of need

Make policies, process guides, FAQs, and contact information easy to locate when employees need them.

↗

Make progress visible

Use a checklist and learning pathway to clarify completed activities and upcoming priorities.

Help & frequently asked questions

Quick answers to common questions during your first few weeks.

Begin with your welcome information and the first-stage checklist. Your manager or onboarding contact can help clarify which activities are most important for your role.
Contact your designated IT support team or onboarding contact. Do not share passwords or sensitive account information when requesting help.
The pathway provides a suggested sequence. Your manager or onboarding coordinator can confirm which activities are mandatory and whether the order needs to change for your role.
Your manager is a good starting point for role expectations, team priorities, and work-related questions. Your onboarding contact can help direct you to other resources.
Onboarding continues beyond the first month. You and your manager can identify further learning needs, set development goals, and plan additional support.
Still need help?

Contact your manager or designated onboarding coordinator for guidance specific to your role and organization.

05 · Evaluation

How could the onboarding experience be evaluated?

The concept would need to be tested with new employees, managers, and onboarding coordinators to understand whether it makes the experience easier to navigate.

Task completion

Can new employees locate key resources, understand their priorities, and complete onboarding activities?

Confidence and clarity

Do employees understand what is expected of them and where to get help when they need it?

Onboarding coordination

Can managers and HR/L&D teams use the structure to communicate expectations and identify outstanding activities?

06 · Reflection

Good onboarding is more than a list of tasks.

FirstSteps explores how user-centred design and adult learning principles can help make the transition into a new role more manageable. By organizing information into stages, connecting learning to practical activities, and making resources easier to access, the experience aims to give employees a clearer sense of direction. The platform supports onboarding, but it does not replace the relationships, conversations, and guidance that help new employees feel part of a team.

Project 03 · UX + Self-Directed Learning

LearnFlow: A More Supportive LMS Experience

A learning management system concept designed to make workplace learning easier to navigate. LearnFlow brings course discovery, learning resources, and progress tracking into one organized experience.

Project type
Independent concept

01 · The challenge

Making workplace learning easier to navigate

Learning platforms can contain valuable training and development resources, but learners may struggle to find relevant courses, locate materials, or understand what they should do next.

The design problem

How might we create a more organized and supportive learning experience that helps employees find relevant learning, manage their progress, and take ownership of their development?

Learner needs

Employees need a clear view of assigned learning, accessible course materials, visible progress, and recommendations that connect to their interests and development goals.

Design opportunity

Bring key learning activities into one place and reduce the effort required to move between course discovery, learning content, and progress information.

02 · Audience & objectives

Designing around the learner

The concept is intended for employees balancing required workplace training with optional professional development.

01

Assigned learners

Employees who need to complete mandatory courses and understand deadlines and outstanding requirements.

02

Self-directed learners

Employees exploring optional courses and resources to build skills or support career development.

03

Busy professionals

Employees who need to quickly locate relevant information and return to learning without unnecessary navigation.

Learning experience objectives

✓Help learners locate assigned and recommended learning opportunities.
✓Make course progress and outstanding learning requirements easy to understand.
✓Connect learners with resources that support their interests and development goals.
✓Provide a consistent place to access courses, materials, and learning activities.
03 · Experience strategy

From course access to learner support

The interface is designed to support self-directed learning by helping employees understand their options, make informed choices, and monitor their progress.

⌂

Centralized dashboard

Bring assigned courses, learning activity, and recommended resources together so learners can see what matters at a glance.

◎

Clear course discovery

Organize learning opportunities by topic and provide concise descriptions, estimated time, and course details before learners begin.

↗

Visible progress

Use progress indicators and completion information to help learners understand where they are and what remains.

✧

Relevant recommendations

Suggest learning based on a learner's stated goals, interests, and previous activity, while keeping recommendations transparent and optional.

05 · Recommendation concept

Making learning recommendations more relevant

A future enhancement could use a recommendation system to help learners discover resources connected to their development goals and previous learning activity.

Information the system could use

Self-selected interests, learning goals, completed courses, topics explored, and optional learner feedback. These signals could help identify relevant next steps.

How recommendations could work

A recommendation engine could compare learner-selected interests with course topics and metadata, then rank relevant resources. More advanced AI could support natural-language discovery and personalized suggestions.

Important design consideration

Recommendations should be explainable, optional, and easy to adjust. The concept does not assume that people have fixed learning styles. Learners should remain in control of what they choose to explore.

06 · Evaluation

How could the experience be evaluated?

The concept would need usability testing and learner feedback before being considered for implementation.

Task completion

Can learners locate an assigned course, find a resource, and identify their next learning activity?

Usability and clarity

Can learners understand navigation labels, progress indicators, course descriptions, and recommendations?

Learner feedback

Do employees find the platform easier to navigate and the recommended resources relevant to their goals?

07 · Reflection

Good learning experiences need more than good content.

This project explores how user-centred design can support workplace learning beyond the course itself. By making learning easier to find, understand, and manage, an LMS can reduce navigation friction and help employees take a more active role in their development. The interface supports the learning experience; it does not replace thoughtful instructional design.

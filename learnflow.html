Project 03 · UX + self-directed learning

LearnFlow: A More Supportive LMS Experience

Rethinking the learning management system experience through clearer navigation, personalized recommendations, and progress visibility. A concept designed to help learners take a more active role in their development.

UX/UI Design Figma User Flows Wireframes Interactive Prototyping
01 / Project overview

Making workplace learning easier to navigate

The learning problem

Learners can lose time navigating a learning platform, locating materials, and figuring out what to do next. When courses, resources, and progress information are spread across different areas, it can be difficult to stay organized and motivated. LearnFlow explores how a clearer, more supportive interface can reduce this friction.

Target audience

Employees completing assigned workplace learning as well as learners exploring optional development opportunities. The concept considers people with different experience levels, schedules, and learning goals.

01 Find assigned and recommended learning in one place.
02 Understand course progress, completion status, and next steps.
03 Discover resources that relate to personal and professional goals.

Why self-directed learning?

Self-directed learning gives learners more visibility and choice in their development. LearnFlow supports this by making learning opportunities easier to discover, progress easier to understand, and next steps easier to identify. The platform supports the learning process rather than dictating how someone must learn.

04 / How the AI feature could work

From learner information to relevant recommendations

In a developed LMS, AI-assisted recommendations could be supported by a combination of learner preferences, course information, and learning activity. The following outlines a possible approach.

STEP 01

Understand learner goals

Learners identify skills they want to develop, such as communication, leadership, or technical knowledge. They can update these goals whenever their priorities change.

STEP 02

Identify interests

Learners select subjects and topics they want to explore. This gives the platform additional context beyond assigned workplace training.

STEP 03

Consider learning activity

With appropriate permission, the system can consider completed courses, topics explored, progress, and quiz results to help identify useful next steps.

STEP 04

Suggest relevant resources

An AI-assisted recommendation service could match learner information with course descriptions, skills, and learning objectives to suggest relevant resources.

How could the recommendations adapt over time?

Imagine a learner who wants to improve communication and completes a course on effective teamwork. The platform could then suggest a lesson on active listening, a practice activity on giving constructive feedback, or a course on managing difficult conversations. If the learner changes their goal to leadership, the recommendations could shift toward coaching, delegation, and team development.

The goal is not to assign learners a fixed learning style or decide what they must study. It is to make useful resources easier to discover while keeping the learner in control.

05 / Design and evaluation

Designing an experience that supports independent learning

UX design decisions

A centralized dashboard, consistent navigation, visible progress indicators, and organized course cards help learners understand where they are and what they can do next.

Learning experience design

Course recommendations and progress cues support self-directed learning. Content can be organized into manageable lessons, with clear objectives and feedback that help learners understand their progress.

Assessment and evaluation

Usability testing could ask representative learners to find a course, locate a resource, and identify their next step. Measures could include task completion, errors, time on task, and learner feedback.

Privacy and responsible AI

A future recommendation system should use relevant learner information transparently, provide appropriate privacy controls, and allow learners to adjust their preferences or dismiss recommendations. AI suggestions should support, not replace, learner choice.

Project status: This project presents a UX and learning experience design concept for a more supportive LMS. The interface demonstrates proposed dashboard features, course navigation, progress tracking, and a simulated personalized recommendation experience. The recommendation demo uses predefined rules rather than a live AI integration. Future development could connect an approved AI service to learner goals, interests, and learning activity to generate more tailored suggestions.

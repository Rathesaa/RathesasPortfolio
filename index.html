Learn it. Try it. Apply it.
Good learning design is more than presenting information. It helps people understand, practise, and use what they learn.

My background combines UX/UI design, visual communication, and adult education. I’m interested in the point where design and learning meet: how people find information, make sense of it, build confidence, and apply it in real situations.

In my UX/UI work at Hitachi Rail, I’ve contributed to user research, usability testing, wireframes, prototypes, workflows, and technical documentation. That experience shapes how I approach learning: start with the people using it, make complex ideas easier to navigate, and test whether the experience is clear.

Through my adult education studies and independent learning-design projects, I’m building practical experience with instructional strategies, learning activities, assessment, and digital learning tools.

My learning-design perspective

I don’t assume everyone has one fixed “learning style.” I consider learners’ prior knowledge, goals, context, accessibility needs, and preferences—then choose a mix of methods that supports understanding and practice.

My approach

Choose the method to fit the learning goal.

Different methods serve different purposes. I think about what learners need to know or do, then select an approach that gives them the right kind of support and practice.

Scenario-based learning

Places learners in realistic situations where they make decisions and see the consequences.

Useful for: judgement and decision-making

Retrieval practice

Asks learners to recall information instead of only rereading or reviewing it.

Useful for: strengthening recall

Spaced practice

Revisits important ideas over time, rather than presenting everything in one session.

Useful for: longer-term retention

Worked examples

Shows a model or completed example before asking learners to solve a similar task.

Useful for: building foundational skills

Scaffolding

Breaks a complex task into manageable steps, then gradually reduces support as confidence grows.

Useful for: learning new processes

Reflection and discussion

Gives learners space to connect new ideas with experience and consider alternative perspectives.

Useful for: insight and application
From need to evaluation

A thoughtful design process.

I use a practical, learner-centred process and adapt it to the scope, audience, and goals of each project.

01

Understand

Identify the audience, context, existing knowledge, task, and performance gap.

02

Design

Set measurable objectives and choose content, practice, and assessment methods.

03

Develop

Create accessible content, prototypes, activities, and job aids; gather feedback and refine.

04

Evaluate

Check understanding and usability, then consider whether learners can apply the skill at work.

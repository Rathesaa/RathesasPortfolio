Good learning design is more than presenting information. It helps people understand, practise, and use what they learn.

My background brings together UX/UI design, technical communication, visual design, and adult education. I’m interested in how people find information, make sense of complex processes, build confidence, and apply new knowledge in real situations.

In my current role at Hitachi Rail, I contribute to technical documentation, HMI manuals, project learning materials, and course reviews. My UX/UI work also includes user research, usability testing, wireframes, prototypes, and workflows. These experiences have taught me to consider the people using information, make complex ideas easier to navigate, and look for ways to improve clarity and usability.

I completed a Certificate in Adult Education at Conestoga College, with studies in adult learning, curriculum development, assessment, evaluation, and instructional techniques. I apply this knowledge through independent learning-design projects and digital prototypes.

My learning-design perspective

Effective learning starts with the learner and the task. I consider prior knowledge, goals, context, accessibility, and the type of performance expected, then choose methods that support understanding, practice, feedback, and application.

Learning methods

Choose the method to fit the learning goal.

Different methods serve different purposes. I consider what learners need to know or do, then select an approach that provides the right kind of explanation, support, practice, and feedback.

Scenario-based learning

Places learners in realistic situations where they make decisions and consider the consequences.

Useful for: judgement and decision-making

Retrieval practice

Asks learners to recall and use information instead of only rereading or reviewing it.

Useful for: strengthening recall

Spaced practice

Revisits important ideas over time rather than presenting everything in one session.

Useful for: longer-term retention

Worked examples

Shows a model or completed example before asking learners to solve a similar task.

Useful for: building foundational skills

Scaffolding

Breaks a complex task into manageable steps, gradually reducing support as confidence grows.

Useful for: learning new processes

Reflection and discussion

Connects new ideas with experience and gives learners space to consider alternative perspectives.

Useful for: insight and application
My process

From learning need to evaluation.

I use a practical, learner-centred process and adapt it to the scope, audience, and goals of each project.

01

Understand

Identify the audience, context, existing knowledge, task, and performance gap.

02

Design

Set measurable objectives and choose content, practice, and assessment methods.

03

Develop

Create accessible content, prototypes, activities, and job aids; gather feedback and refine.

04

Evaluate

Check understanding and usability, then consider whether learners can apply the skill at work.

Conflict Management: Choosing Your Response

An interactive workplace communication simulation that gives employees an opportunity to practise responding to challenging conversations, consider different perspectives, and reflect on the impact of their communication choices.

The design challenge

Knowing what to say is different from knowing how to respond.

Conflict can arise from misunderstandings, competing priorities, unclear expectations, or differences in perspective. Employees need opportunities to practise communicating constructively when conversations become difficult.

How might we help employees practise responding to workplace conflict with empathy, clarity, and professionalism?
Project scope

An independent learning design concept using fictional workplace situations. It is intended for communication practice, not formal mediation or conflict-resolution training.

Workplace conflict is not always caused by a major disagreement. It can develop through missed communication, unclear responsibilities, competing deadlines, or assumptions about another person's actions.

Employees may know that active listening and respectful communication are important, but applying those skills during a tense conversation can be challenging.

A presentation can explain communication techniques, but it cannot fully recreate the experience of choosing words in a difficult conversation. For this reason, I selected a branching scenario approach that allows learners to make choices and see how different responses may influence the interaction.

The simulation follows a fictional workplace disagreement between two colleagues. Learners can explore different approaches and consider the possible consequences without real-world pressure.

Audience and learner needs

Designed for everyday workplace conversations.

Intended audience

Employees who collaborate with colleagues, customers, or stakeholders and may need to navigate disagreements, misunderstandings, or challenging conversations.

Learning challenge

Learners may understand communication principles in theory but struggle to choose a constructive response when they feel challenged, misunderstood, or under pressure.

Potential barriers

  • Feeling defensive or misunderstood.
  • Making assumptions about another person's intentions.
  • Responding emotionally rather than listening.
  • Difficulty expressing concerns clearly.

Design response

  • Use realistic workplace dialogue.
  • Offer more than one plausible response.
  • Show potential consequences without shaming learners.
  • Include reflection and opportunities to retry.
Learning objectives

What should learners be able to do?

The objectives focus on observable communication behaviours that can be practised in a simulated conversation.

01

Recognize communication behaviours that may escalate or de-escalate a workplace disagreement.

02

Select responses that demonstrate active listening, respect, and a willingness to understand another perspective.

03

Communicate a concern using clear, specific, and professional language.

04

Identify when a disagreement may require additional support, guidance, or escalation through appropriate workplace channels.

Instructional strategy

Learn by making choices and reflecting on them.

The simulation is designed to make communication decisions visible. Learners explore different approaches, receive feedback, and consider how context influences the next step.

01

Branching scenarios

Learners select from different responses and see how each choice may influence the direction of a conversation.

Supports: decision-making practice
02

Experiential learning

Learners experience a situation, consider the outcome, and reflect on how they might respond differently.

Supports: practical application
03

Reflective feedback

Feedback explains the potential impact of a response and offers a different perspective to consider.

Supports: self-awareness and reflection
Scenario design

A conversation that develops through learner choices.

The scenario is organized into stages so learners can first understand the issue, then practise listening, communicating a concern, and agreeing on a next step.

Simulation complete · Reflection

Take a moment to reflect.

You have completed the conversation simulation. Review the communication approaches you explored and consider how your choices influenced the interaction.

Your conversation summary

1. Listening before responding

Did you take time to understand Jordan's concern before explaining your own perspective?

2. Focusing on the issue

Did your response address the communication problem rather than assigning blame or making assumptions?

3. Agreeing on next steps

Did you identify an action that could help prevent the same misunderstanding in the future?

Remember

Constructive communication does not mean agreeing with everything another person says. It means listening, communicating respectfully, clarifying the issue, and working toward an appropriate next step.

Learning reinforcement

Communication strategies to take away.

The simulation reinforces practical behaviours that can be used in everyday workplace conversations.

01

Listen actively

Give the other person time to explain their concern. Ask questions to understand rather than immediately preparing a defence.

02

Focus on specifics

Describe observable situations and their impact instead of making assumptions about someone's intentions.

03

Agree on next steps

Where appropriate, identify a practical action, clarify responsibilities, and agree on how to communicate going forward.

Assessment and evaluation

How could learning be evaluated?

The purpose of this simulation is to encourage thoughtful communication, not to grade employees on whether they choose one supposedly perfect response.

Decision-making

Review whether learners can identify responses that demonstrate listening, clarification, respectful language, and problem-solving.

Reflection and confidence

Ask learners to reflect on their choices and indicate which communication strategies they feel more prepared to use.

Workplace application

Use follow-up feedback or appropriate manager observations to explore whether learners apply constructive communication behaviours in relevant situations.

Important evaluation consideration

Conflict is influenced by context, workplace culture, power dynamics, and individual circumstances. Evaluation should not assume that every disagreement can or should be resolved through one conversation. Some situations require a manager, HR, a formal process, or other appropriate support.

Design reflection

What this project demonstrates.

Practising a conversation in a safe environment can help learners consider their responses before facing similar situations at work.

This project explores how branching scenarios can be used to practise interpersonal skills that are difficult to learn through information alone.

Rather than presenting one perfect answer, the simulation demonstrates how different communication choices can affect the direction of a conversation. Feedback focuses on the potential impact of a response and encourages learners to consider alternative approaches.

A future version could include additional scenarios involving customer concerns, competing priorities, and conversations with different levels of authority. It would also benefit from subject-matter expert review and learner testing.

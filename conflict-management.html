Project 02 · Branching scenario · Experiential practice

Conflict Management: Choosing Your Response

A workplace communication learning experience where employees practise navigating challenging conversations, explore different responses, and reflect on how their choices influence an interaction.

Branching scenarios Scenario writing AI-inspired feedback Reflection-based learning
01 / Project overview

Learning to navigate difficult conversations

The learning problem

Knowing communication tips is not the same as being able to use them in a tense conversation. Employees need opportunities to practise listening, choosing their words, and responding thoughtfully in a realistic but low-risk environment.

Target audience

Employees who communicate with coworkers, customers, or other stakeholders. Scenarios can be adapted to different workplace responsibilities and organizational contexts.

01 Recognize communication choices that may escalate or de-escalate a disagreement.
02 Select responses that demonstrate listening and respectful communication.
03 Reflect on how tone, timing, and word choice can affect an interaction.

Why use branching scenarios?

Branching scenarios provide experiential practice. Learners make a choice, see a plausible consequence, and consider an alternative approach. Rather than memorizing a list of communication rules, they practise applying judgement in context.

02 / Interactive learning experience

What would you do?

Explore a workplace disagreement. Choose how you would respond, review how the conversation might develop, and consider another approach. There is no single perfect phrase for every conflict.

05 / Learning design decisions

Designing for thoughtful communication

Assessment approach

Learners review the consequences of their choices and explain why a response may help or hinder a conversation. Reflection focuses on observable behaviours such as listening, clarifying, and acknowledging another person's perspective.

AI-inspired personalization

A learning assistant provides targeted suggestions based on the learner's chosen development area. A future version could use approved AI tools to generate additional examples and adapt practice activities while maintaining appropriate privacy and organizational safeguards.

Accessibility and inclusion

The experience uses plain language, clear navigation, readable contrast, and multiple opportunities to explore alternative responses. Workplace scenarios should account for different communication styles and power dynamics.

Design reflection

Conflict situations are nuanced. The activity should not suggest that every situation has one perfect response. Some situations require a pause, a manager's support, or formal workplace processes.

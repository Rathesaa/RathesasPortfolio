Conflict Management: Choosing Your Response

An interactive practice concept where learners navigate a challenging workplace conversation and consider how different responses affect the situation.

At a glance

eXeLearning concept · Branching design · Scenario writing · Reflection prompts

The learning problem

Knowing communication tips is not the same as being able to use them in a tense conversation. Learners need a safe way to practise considering their options.

Target audience

Employees who communicate with coworkers, customers, or other stakeholders. The scenarios should be adapted to the role and workplace context.

Learning objectives

By the end of this learning experience, learners should be able to:

  • Recognize communication choices that may escalate or de-escalate a disagreement.
  • Select a response that demonstrates listening and respectful communication.
  • Reflect on how tone and word choice can affect an interaction.

Why this learning method?

Branching scenarios provide experiential practice: learners make a choice, see a plausible consequence, and reflect before trying another approach. Reflection prompts connect the activity to prior experience.

Design and development

The concept begins with a workplace disagreement. Learners choose how to respond, then follow a short branch showing how the conversation might progress. Each branch includes feedback and a prompt to consider another approach.

Assessment and evaluation

Learners explain why a response may help or hinder the conversation. A more developed version could use a scoring rubric tied to observable communication behaviours rather than treating one exact phrase as the only correct answer.

Design reflection

Conflict situations are nuanced, so the activity should avoid implying that every situation has one perfect response. The branches should model constructive options while acknowledging context, power dynamics, and when to seek support.
